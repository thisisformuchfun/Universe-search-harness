# Literature and original 14: 2026-11-02 UTC

The [14-source evidence matrix](../held14-rv-recovery-20261102T0622Z-01a193cc/SOURCE_MATRIX.md)
remains the source-specific status: 14 held; two previously published as
compact-binary candidates, 12 with novelty unresolved, and no new independent
inner orbit or black-hole discovery. This note changes no disposition or policy.

* [Li et al., arXiv v1, 26 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34715v1)
  explicitly list Gaia DR3 4491401821468887552 and 824344003814438528 among
  their candidates with one effective RV epoch each. These are known literature
  controls, not discoveries here. The full machine-readable list was not
  available in the earlier investigation, so the other 12 cannot be declared
  novel from the printed excerpt.
* [El-Badry et al., arXiv v2, 8 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.06453v2)
  report extensive RV follow-up, luminous companions and hierarchical triples
  in their selected samples. Their discussion shows why even a single-lined
  spectrum may fail to exclude an inner luminous binary; source-specific flux
  and line-profile sensitivity are needed. Those sample fractions are not
  calibrated false-alarm probabilities for our 14. The earlier attempt to read
  the full [CaltechDATA deposit](https://data.caltech.edu/records/0jxb3-ny611)
  by byte range received HTTP 403; no deposit nonmatch or extra RV epoch can be
  inferred. No access restriction was bypassed.
* [Marcussen et al., arXiv v1, 8 September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08590)
  report 13 near-twin stellar binaries in a selected sample of 20 Gaia
  substellar-companion candidates, using spectroscopic vetting including
  line-profile morphology. This different population does not supply a rate or
  identify the nature of any of our compact-object candidates. It motivates
  seeking component-sensitive spectra rather than assuming a dark companion.
* [Holl et al., arXiv v4, 31 May 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.11971)
  discuss Gaia scan-angle-related spurious periods. The matrix records several
  ~31-day solutions as a concern; period proximity by itself does not prove an
  individual orbit is spurious. Independent, phase-resolved RVs would test
  orbital consistency, but must be attributable to the target and checked for
  instrument offsets, blends and line-profile alternatives.

The nearby Gaia/PS1 sources and blend flags described in the matrix motivate
source-attribution and resolved-light checks; neither proves an associated
triple nor quantifies contamination. For 1533365584518328576, the existing
single low-S/N LAMOST redshift and PS1 wing do not establish an independent
orbit or the spectrum's source. Decisive progress requires suitable independent,
calibrated repeated spectroscopy and/or validated resolved observations; no
observations or additional archive requests are scheduled here.
